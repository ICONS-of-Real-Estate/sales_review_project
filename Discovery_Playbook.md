# Discovery Playbook

> Companion to `Phase2_CallGradingSOP.md` and the discovery rubric in `Phase2_CallScoring.gs` (`discoveryRubricPrompt_` / `goalAndPainRubricPrompt_` / `DISCOVERY_GAP_LABELS_`). Built from Tomás's own method, taught live on the 08/09/2026 training call with Joana. Tomás approves changes here before they're used in training, same as the objection-handling playbooks.

## What the rubric grades

Five things, each scored independently — a call can pass some and fail others, and the session should go at whichever one is actually failing, not at "discovery" as one undifferentiated blob.

| Flag | The question |
|---|---|
| `discovery_adequate` | Did the rep ask real questions **before** pitching? Open questions that got the lead talking about their own business. Rhetorical set-ups for the pitch don't count. |
| `understood_leads_business` | By the end, had the rep demonstrably **grasped** what this lead does — judged on them referring back to specifics the lead gave, not on whether they asked. |
| `confirmed_prior_discovery` | On a call following an earlier QC: did they **confirm** what that call surfaced and go **deeper** where it was thin? Both, not either. Automatically true on genuine first contact. |
| `uncovered_goal` | What is this lead running **towards**? |
| `uncovered_pain` | What is this lead running **from**? |

### The goal/pain distinction — read this before coaching it

Added 08/09/2026 because it's the half the rubric was missing. Tomás's framing:

> "Find the goal. Find the pain. Where are they running towards, and where are they running from?"
> "If I know where you're going, I know where to meet you, and I know how I can take you there."

**The trap is the pain half.** The obvious answer is wrong, and Tomás corrected it explicitly on the call:

> "The pain of being stuck at 5 calls — it's not particularly a pain. It's like a disease, it's a condition. The pain that they have is something more peculiar. For example: not having any presence on social media, not on Google. When you Google them, you don't find a professional profile. Not active on Instagram. Content lacking engagement. Don't have a big enough network in the community. Not well known in the community. That's where we can help."

So "only selling 5 houses a month" is a **condition** — the lead has lived with it for years and it isn't what makes them act. A real pain is specific, named in the lead's own words, and is something a podcast could plausibly address:

- No professional presence when someone Googles them
- Nothing on social, or content that gets no engagement
- A network too small or too shallow in their own community
- Not being known or taken seriously locally
- Can't have qualified recruiting conversations because nobody rates them
- Losing listings to agents with a bigger profile

A rep who only established the numeric gap **fails** `uncovered_pain`, however many questions they asked about it.

The goal half is simpler but still needs specificity — "wants to grow" is not a goal, "wants to go from 5 a month to 10 by spring" is. Kris's line of questioning for pressure-testing it:

> "How long have you been at 5 houses? Are you really motivated to get to 10, or did you just pull that number out of your ass? What stopped you getting there before? If you've got that goal, why aren't you achieving it right now?"

## Tomás's method

Taught live on 08/09/2026 using Xavier Long — a real lead Joana had a sales call booked with that same day.

### 1. Do the due diligence before the call

In a few minutes of searching, Tomás found that Xavier: has ~600,000 TikTok followers, is a founding member of the Ethos Alabama Birmingham expansion team, sells cars alongside real estate, and was a competitive boxer. Joana went in knowing almost none of it.

> **Tomás:** "Do you know what his plan is on TikTok? On how he's going to leverage his half a million followers?"
> **Joana:** "Not really."
> **Kris:** "If the dude knows how to get half a million followers, and he's brand new to real estate, he's gonna crush it."

Where to look: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Zillow profile, and the brokerage's own announcement posts. Brokerage posts in particular hand you something to press on — Xavier's said he was "part of the first group helping build our Birmingham expansion team", which is either a real recruiting remit or fancy words, and that's a question:

> **Tomás:** "Are you going to be tasked with recruiting agents? What does this mean? We can only ever sell someone, and pitch someone, once we found the full scope of who they are as a real estate agent, and what they are trying to accomplish."

### 2. Write the questions down before the call

> **Tomás:** "Note down a few questions. Don't keep it in your mind."

### 3. Take notes during the call, visibly

> **Tomás:** "Active listening is super important, but do take notes... people would even be curious — 'oh wow, she's actually taking notes' — and not just asking empty questions so she can go into the pitch."
> **Tomás:** "I have sticky notes. Even something quite relevant, I write down if I want to mention it 10 minutes later — because if you try to remember it, it's gonna slip away."

### 4. Open a second/sales call by confirming, not re-asking

Never re-run the QC's discovery from scratch. The opener:

> **Tomás:** "Hey X, this is from our previous call, this is what I uncovered, just want to confirm that... and then I have some further questions that are gonna help me understand the full scope of your business. Is it alright if I go through them right now?"

Time has passed since the QC, so the confirm is also an update:

> **Tomás:** "You closed your friend's transaction just recently — how many more do you have? Do you have 3 more? 5 more? You can even joke with them: 'oh, 10 more? Finally got your 10?'"

Tomás holds himself to this too:

> "Sometimes trying to get ahold of myself on second calls, I should prepare a little bit better, because sometimes I may ask questions that we already know. That shouldn't happen."

### 5. Qualify the mentality, not just the budget

The "top producing agents" frame, for a lead who looks too new or too small:

> **Tomás:** "Usually we only work with top producing agents. It's not because of our pricing — that remains accessible. It is more because of the mentality of top producing agents, of wanting to build a brand that feeds their business, instead of waking up at 5am and coming back at 8pm every day. So it's not that you can't do a podcast, but it will depend on your mentality. Is this a long-term play for you? Is this a cash-and-grab situation? Because if it is a cash-and-grab, we can't help you — we'd be building a brand for something that's not going to exist in 2 years."

### 6. Qualify budget before showing the offer

> **Tomás:** "Before I even show you the strategy... this call is free, I won't charge you, I promise. But the service that we do, it is not. So just to understand — what would be a budget? Just throw a number. If he says $50 or $10, you can just say, 'hey, I'm not gonna show you the goodies if that's all you have.' And just be honest with them. That will save you some time."

### 7. Name a focus mismatch instead of ignoring it

Xavier sells cars as well as houses. Rather than skating past it:

> **Tomás:** "People don't want to buy bananas from people that grow apples. We are working with a very competitive market, and you're fighting against people that are full-time on real estate. And the same thing with cars. What is your plan? What do you plan to achieve in the near future?"
> **Tomás:** "We've really never done a podcast to help people sell cars — but if you want a podcast to help you sell houses, that's what we live for. So is that something that you're looking for?"

Then ask the direct question **before** pitching: *is this something you're looking for?*

## Drills

1. **First 90 seconds, twice.** Once opening with the pitch (the failure mode), once opening with "walk me through what a typical week looks like for you right now". Compare how much the lead volunteers.
2. **Recall test.** After a discovery block, have the rep summarise the lead's business from memory with no notes. Anything they can't summarise was asked but not absorbed — that's `understood_leads_business` failing while `discovery_adequate` passes.
3. **Goal vs. pain sort.** Read out five things a lead said; have the rep sort each into goal / pain / neither. Anything they file as a pain that's really a condition ("only doing 5 a month") gets challenged.
4. **Due diligence, timed.** Give the rep a real upcoming lead and 5 minutes. What did they find, and what three questions does it produce?
5. **Confirm-and-deepen opener.** Hand them a one-paragraph fake QC summary; have them open a mock second call confirming it and going one layer deeper, in under 30 seconds — without re-asking anything already in the summary.
