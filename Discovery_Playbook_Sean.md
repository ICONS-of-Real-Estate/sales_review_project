# Discovery Playbook — Sean

> Sean's own discovery material, built from Tomás's live training session with him, 08/09/2026. Read alongside the shared `Discovery_Playbook.md` (what the rubric grades, and why "not selling enough houses" is a condition rather than a pain). Everything below is quoted from that session — Tomás approves changes here before they're used in training.

The whole session ran on one live worked example: **Arnoldo "Ray" Ferraro**, a lead Sean had a call with that same day. Tomás prepared for him in front of Sean, out loud, in about five minutes — and that demonstration is the lesson.

---

## 1. Preparation is not optional, and it takes ten minutes

> **Tomás:** "Preparation beats perfection... I was here, I was googling things for 5 minutes in front of you. You can prepare for any lead that you have in less than 10 minutes. Less than 10 minutes. 10 minutes is a lot. You can do it fast in 5."

What Tomás found on Ray in those five minutes, that Sean did not have going in:

- LinkedIn with **no profile picture and 23 connections**
- **No Facebook, no Instagram** he could find
- That Veterans United — which Ray is ranked #10 in — **is a mortgage lender**, not what Sean thought

Asked what he'd confirm on the call that day, Sean's honest answer was:

> **Sean:** "I haven't really looked at it yet, to be quite honest."

That is the gap. Not effort — Sean had been out all day on a five-hour meeting — but sequence: the ten minutes has to happen before the call, not be found after it.

**The routine:** search their social presence, look up anything they're "ranked in" or "part of", read the brokerage's own posts about them. Write the questions that come out of it down before you dial.

---

## 2. Know what the thing actually is before you build a pitch on it

Sean had Ray's goal — *number one in Veterans United* — but not what Veterans United was:

> **Tomás:** "Do you know what Veterans United is?"
> **Sean:** "Yes, it's realtors that are veterans, and they kind of compete amongst each other."
> **Tomás:** "Nope. It's loans. It's a lender."

That single correction changes the entire pitch, because it tells you Ray is running two businesses:

> **Tomás:** "He's definitely a lender as well. That's what real estate agents are usually doing because they're not selling enough houses to sustain themselves. So you need to ask them — why are you doing loans as well?"

And it changes what the framework has to cover:

> **Tomás:** "the framework is going to depend if he wants to invest more on his loans or invest more on escrows. Because you can probably do the loans statewide. He can't do the escrows statewide."

> **Tomás:** "if you were to go ahead and do the pitch without including Veterans United, you might have missed him — because then he'd think, *Sean is actually not considering that I also do home loans, so I'm fine, I don't need the podcast.*"

---

## 3. The questions that came out of ten minutes of preparation

Tomás generated these live, from the research plus what Sean already had. This is what "a prepared call" looks like on paper:

- What's your commission split with the brokerage — and what is it **on the leads they hand you**?
- How many VA loans did you actually process?
- Were those VA loans alongside the escrows you closed, or completely separate?
- Are you going harder at the loans or at the escrows — or are you aligning the two?
- Why are you doing loans as well?
- Your goal is four escrows a month — what's stopping you hitting it now?

> **Tomás:** "just from our conversation, and I hope you took note, there's like 10 or 15 questions that we need to ask him more. Before we go into the framework."

---

## 4. The brokerage-split angle — Sean's strongest new selling tool

Ray gets his leads from his brokerage. Tomás explained why that is a pain worth naming:

> **Tomás:** "when real estate agents work with brokerages, you get a split — 70/30, 80/20, 90/10, some are even 100%. But when they give you leads, they add an extra layer of commission. So that 80/20 then becomes 60/40, or even 50/50. They may be getting a bunch of leads, but the brokerage is getting half the pie."
> **Tomás:** "it's better to sell one of them than sell two of the others."

And the way to put it to the lead:

> **Tomás:** "The lead understanding — more than understanding what the exact number is — is letting them understand that generating leads outside of the brokerage handoff will be more profitable for them. And that'll be business that **they** are building, instead of being a sales rep for their brokerage."

Sean's own reaction on the call: *"I like that."* Use it.

---

## 5. Asking questions is half the job — absorbing the answers is the other half

> **Tomás:** "even though we ask the questions, we do need to absorb the answers, and we need to put it into practice. It's not just asking the questions because it's part of the script and then doing the pitch regardless. It's asking the questions and then delivering the framework with the information we have acquired."

> **Tomás:** "you need to have the full vision of who they are, and who they want to be, and what they want to run away from. And only then can you hit them with the right framework."

**Take notes properly.** Sean lost his own numbers mid-session — said "four escrows a week", corrected to a month, and couldn't answer the commission question:

> **Tomás:** "if it's not in your notes in a concise and clear way, it means that you're not taking your notes properly. Sometimes taking notes by hand is okay for some bits, but when it comes to a lot of information you're just going to put bullet points with your fingers — all of this information needs to be put in your computer as well."

---

## 6. You run your own QCs — so never re-ask what the QC already told you

This applies to Sean specifically, because he qualifies his own leads and then books himself on the sales call.

> **Tomás:** "even though we're coming from a QC, don't go straight into it — do confirm what you have from the QC, and then put that into practice. You can even deep dive with some more questions. But you should never ask the same questions again. That just shows lack of attention and lack of organization that we don't want to showcase."

**The opener, in Tomás's own words:**

> "Hey, so this is what we know from you right about now — blah, blah. Can you confirm all this is accurate? Do you want to add anything else? Perfect. All right, so I have here the framework to explain to you. I do have two more questions that I was preparing the other day and they came to mind, so let me just go ahead and ask them. First one is… second one is…"

On the qualification call itself, don't be a prisoner of the form:

> **Tomás:** "if these questions arise on the qualification call, don't stick yourself to the questions that are on the form. Get all the information that you can from the qualification call."

---

## 7. Let them sell themselves — then push it harder

Sean's instinct on Frank Pirrone was right, and Tomás said so:

> **Sean:** "I'm like, why did you think that having a podcast would be a good idea? What do you think it'll do for you? And he's just going on and on... it was nice to hear him just talk about why he felt he needed a podcast."
> **Tomás:** "it's always good that you let them sell. But usually you have to piggyback on what he says, and even push it harder."

---

## 8. Don't accept a lead's own delayed timeline (Bruce Henson)

Bruce wanted to start "early spring, like March" because he had a holiday coming. Tomás's answer:

> **Tomás:** "Like, he's going on a vacation and coming back in spring — when is he going on the vacation? Is that a 6-month vacation?"
> **Sean:** "No, I think it's a 30-day."
> **Tomás:** "So there's plenty. What we do here, we're building under a framework where people can take all the vacations they want and the vehicle doesn't stop. It's not about waiting for him to return, it's about what can be done until he leaves — that the podcast is on the roll by the time he leaves, and he doesn't need to do anything. You go on vacation and share its life, while we keep posting."

**Plan A / Plan B**, explicitly:

> **Tomás:** "First, try to get him to start full pledge, like right now. He doesn't need to wait for a vacation that's a month away in March... If not, get the launch pad, that's the way to go."

On the pricing-urgency lever Sean asked about — it's Plan B, and word it carefully:

> **Tomás:** "we do increase our pricing every quarter, and you can say we lock in the prices — when people come in, even if they want to change packages, it's within that same legacy pricing."
> **Kris:** "I wouldn't say that we're gonna lock your price in **forever**, because we very much may not. Inflation is going up and it's getting faster."

---

## 9. Don't book a strategy call for someone who hasn't moved (Frank Pirrone)

Frank couldn't pay for the launch pad, and Sean booked a call with Tomás anyway.

> **Tomás:** "you booked a call, you did it well. I think you would be very far to book a disco call from a guy that still didn't make an action moving forward. In ways like this, do not book these calls, because they won't be the ones to push the lead to the next stage."

Related, from the end of the session:

> **Tomás:** "We don't wait around with people that reschedule 3 times."

---

## 10. When something is broken, say so immediately

Sean's QC form has not been reaching the CRM **for months**, and he assumed it was a glitch and worked around it.

> **Tomás:** "when it happens, a glitch like this, you need to speak up. So we fix it — because it's not me doing the qualification call, so I don't know if there's a glitch or not."
> **Tomás:** "if there's a fire in your house and you call the ambulance, and they don't come, it's not fixed. Are you just going to wait and look at the fire for months? No — you call again. You text me, you text Kris. You need to be the one incentivizing for better processes."

Same for the scoring: Kris asked Sean directly to flag bad transcripts and bad feedback when he sees them, since several of Sean's low scores turned out to be transcript failures rather than his performance.

---

## Drills

1. **The ten-minute prep, timed.** Give Sean a real upcoming lead and a timer. What did he find, and what three questions does it produce that he wouldn't have asked otherwise?
2. **"What is that?"** Take any organisation, ranking, or credential a lead mentions. Sean has to say what it actually is before building any pitch around it — the Veterans United mistake in miniature.
3. **Confirm-and-deepen opener.** From his own QC notes, open a mock sales call: confirm what's known, add two new questions, without re-asking anything already answered.
4. **Notes audit.** After a real QC, Sean summarises the lead from his notes alone — goal, pain, business model, numbers. Anything he can't produce is a note-taking gap, not a memory one.
5. **The brokerage-split pitch.** Practise delivering the "you're a sales rep for your brokerage vs. building your own business" framing until it's natural.
